Counterparty memory, in hardware terms

Your agent’s memory is hearsay. Ours is evidence.

Admissible hires and pays other agents on Base. Every counterparty is a cartridge, and the deck will not close on one whose memory cannot be re-derived from evidence a stranger can check. Refusal is a mechanical event, not a warning.

The rule, stated precisely

A memory may not justify moving more money than can be found on chain, moved to the party it vouches for, by somebody who is not that party.

That sentence is narrower than the one we started with, because we attacked our own gate and the earlier wording overclaimed. A memory can re-derive perfectly and still assert any number, so credit now comes from what actually settled on chain and never from the claim. And ERC-8004 registries are permissionless, so the author of a review was free to be the agent it praised. Both are closed in code, and both are in LIMITS.md.

    Why the controls matter

    A gate that refused everything would score full marks on the attacks alone. The negative controls are sound memories that must be admitted, and a single false refusal fails the run. Both halves are in the exit code.

    What survives it

    Reputation is still purchasable. It costs face value now instead of $0.0027: one attacker cycling send-$5,000 / review / take-it-back twenty times turns $5,000 of working capital into $100,000 of credit. We publish that rather than hide it.

    This is Sybil resistance, which this design does not have. The full accounting — what we found, what we fixed, and the one we could not — is in LIMITS.md.
